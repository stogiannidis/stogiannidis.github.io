Edgy title, I know, but bear with me.

In this article, I'm not arguing about whether academic research is bad or getting worse. My point is that the way research is done has become automated and has lost its character.

When I first started my journey in AI research—that was almost 5 years ago—what intrigued me most was the process of how people came up with new ideas. In my case, I was working at a Greek start-up in Athens at the time, which wanted to decrease the cost of API calls to LLMs (e.g., GPT-4 back then), since the bills from LLM providers were on quite an upward trend. In parallel with my work, I was doing my Master's in Computer Science at AUEB. The good part of this Master's programme was that it was focused on research and served as a very nice stepping stone for a graduate to test the waters of research. Therefore, as a young (and perhaps naive) individual who wanted to explore his interests, I joined it. Just like that, I started exploring how I could bridge Academia and Industry for a common research direction. Of course, I couldn't. That's totally understandable—I was just taking my first steps as a baby in the research world. How could I possibly know how to find my bearings, let alone bridge those gaps?

This is where I got my baptism of fire in how research is done. I sat down in a meeting with my academic and industry supervisors as they were discussing ideas, observations, and thoughts (prayers were most likely a part of it as well). That young kid was absolutely astonished. I had never before seen people so interested in what they were doing, exchanging knowledge. It was like dancing seamlessly under the stars of wisdom, and it kept getting better as the discussion progressed. Within an hour, we had exchanged more information and questions than I could keep track of and write down, yet we narrowed it down to a single project we wanted to tackle. This is where I was thrown to the wolves and asked to find out whether our idea was possible, what other people had done, and how to shape it into its final form. That's where young Ilias fell in love with research—a love that would lead me into marriage (aka starting a PhD).

I started my PhD 2 years ago. That was right before LLMs and AI were that good at coding, "reasoning," and all these tasks people use them for these days. I also think it was before Claude Code was even released (though I'm not sure and I don't really want to check). At the beginning, this marriage was full of love and passion, and it led to our first baby. That, of course, is my first paper in this journey (shameless plug here). Everything was going well until a third party entered our relationship. Its name? AI agents. The relationship suddenly and utterly changed. Ideation became talking to a soulless entity that would either endorse every ill-posed solution or condemn every creative one with potential (you're choosing your poison based on the prompt). Asking a more experienced person for their view on your idea? You’ll get responses such as: "Yeah, have you tried using Claude? What did it say?" or, "If you prompt it well, it will tell you if your idea is good or bad—it knows." The worst-affected part is the one I like the most (younger me reading this sentence would have laughed so much): writing. When I first wrote a paper, I wrote it myself. The tools I used were mostly Grammarly and ChatGPT web, though I didn't like how it wrote things, so I used it mostly to improve my English since I'm not a native speaker. Nowadays, you just copy-paste your experimental results into Claude or ChatGPT (or even Gemini if you're brave enough) and ask it to write the paper in "an academic manner" or using a writing skill (can't stop laughing while writing this). There is no storytelling, no personal touch, no character, not even an individual's mistakes. Everything I read nowadays has the same tone, taste (or perhaps the lack of it), and is so f***ing mundane. I feel like my marriage has turned into a toxic situationship built entirely on convenience.

Don't get me wrong—as a PhD candidate in AI, I'm all for using these tools to handle the grunt work. I have Cursor, Claude Code, and Antigravity (daring, aren't I?) open daily, but I refuse to outsource the thinking. I tried, and every confident, plausible piece of text drained a little more of my soul. Speed isn't the same thing as progress. If we automate away the struggle, the voice, and the taste, what's actually left to fall in love with.
